# DE Zoomcamp sponsor case studies: 2025–2026

**Snapshot:** 10 November 2026. This is a standalone sponsor research brief based on the completed audience and repository audits. It is not media-kit copy.

## Shared evidence base

| Measure | Verified result | What it describes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Final-project authors | 973 unique authors: 366 in 2025, 613 in 2026, with 6 in both cohorts | Distinct CMP student IDs across all submitted final-project attempts |
| Public profile coverage | 631 of 973 (64.9%): 603 high-confidence and 28 medium-confidence profiles | Only these profiles are used in the audience aggregates |
| Excluded or unavailable profiles | 110 low-confidence or rejected matches excluded; 232 with no usable public data | No uncertain identity matches are attributed |
| Named current employers | 395 of 631 profiles (62.6%), across 374 normalized employers | Public profile declarations at collection time, not independently verified employment |
| Independent and contract work | 71 profiles (11.3% of enriched profiles) | Rule-based freelance, self-employed, contractor, or independent-consultant classification |

Among the 395 named employers, 108 are classified as enterprise (27.3%), 74 as agency or consultancy (18.7%), 54 as other (13.7%), 6 as startup or scaleup (1.5%), and 153 remain unknown (38.7%). Industry metadata is available for 328 employer profiles; the largest identified groups are IT services or consulting (67), software or cloud (41), finance or insurance (35), and healthcare or life sciences (30). Sixty-seven employer profiles have unknown industry. Unsupported businesses remain unknown rather than being assumed to be startups.

Seniority is known for 592 enriched profiles. The distribution is 388 mid-level, 70 lead or above, 43 senior, 27 junior, 32 students, and 32 job seekers. Students and job seekers together are 64 of 592 (10.8%).

Role family is known for 565 profiles: 198 data engineering, 116 software engineering, 99 data analytics, 88 data science or machine learning, 27 analytics engineering, and 37 other. The remaining 66 enriched profiles have no classified role family.

Authors with a recorded location span 87 countries. The largest recorded groups are the United States (67), Germany (48), India (37), Indonesia (26), and Nigeria (24). These are profile-location counts, not a representative survey of all course participants.

Among 380 profiles with a dated LinkedIn role start, 189 roles began before the cohort window, 40 during it, and 151 after it. These dates do not establish a job change, course completion, or an outcome caused by the course.

## Astronomer: audience and employer context

The audience audit identifies a global group of practitioners and aspiring practitioners building final projects, with 71 profiles classified as independent or contract workers and 395 profiles naming an employer. Among those named employers, the verified employer list includes EPAM Systems (4 profiles), Accenture (4), Tata Consultancy Services (3), Google (3), Devoteam (3), and Nokia (2). Other sponsor-relevant affiliations in the source review include Oracle, Bloomberg, Huawei, BMW Group, and Coca-Cola Canada Bottling.

This supports an audience-reach story across enterprise and consulting environments. It does not show that these employers use Astronomer, that an author has purchasing authority, or that a sponsor placement led to product adoption. The employer names are public profile affiliations, not customer references.

## Snowflake: warehouse signals in final-project repositories

A static scan found Snowflake code, dependency, or configuration signals in 9 of 352 scanned repositories in 2025 (3%) and 22 of 609 in 2026 (4%). For context, BigQuery signals appeared in 279 of 352 repositories in 2025 (79%) and 442 of 609 in 2026 (73%); Postgres appeared in 233 (66%) and 348 (57%), respectively. DuckDB appeared in 23 (7%) and 107 (18%).

These are repository counts, not unique learner counts. Repositories can contain signals for more than one warehouse. The scan covers distinct submitted GitHub repositories with scannable source or configuration; it does not verify successful execution or production use.

The cached CMP homework export records 583 unique HW3 submitters in 2025 and 873 in 2026. HW3 participation is not a course-enrollment denominator, a completion rate, or a measure of Snowflake use.

## Method and evidence boundaries

- Audience figures use the latest completed enrichment pass and include only high- and medium-confidence profiles in aggregates. Public, self-declared profile data was prioritized; low-confidence and rejected identity matches were excluded.
- Employer and role fields are dated public-profile observations and rule-based classifications. Employer size and business model remain unknown where unsupported.
- Warehouse labels come from static repository source, dependency, and configuration evidence. README mentions alone do not count. The files retain explicit unknown and unscannable categories.
- No student names, handles, CMP IDs, email addresses, or individual profile links are included in this brief.
- Source figures were taken from the finalized private audience summary and statistics, warehouse summary, and orchestrator audit dated 10 November 2026. The underlying per-author data, repository rows, caches, and spend ledger remain outside this repository.

## Figures not present in the evidence

The reviewed artifacts do not contain total course enrollment or registrations by cohort, verified course-completion or credential-award totals, or a validated job-placement, promotion, salary, or career-transition outcome attributable to the course. They also do not establish production deployment, sustained use of a vendor product, sponsor purchase intent, or sponsorship-driven conversion. Those figures remain open; this brief does not infer or invent them.
